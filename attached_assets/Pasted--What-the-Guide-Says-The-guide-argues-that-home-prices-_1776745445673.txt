## What the Guide Says

The guide argues that home prices are the most-watched housing data point but also the least useful for predicting a downturn, because prices are a lagging indicator. By the time they fall, the sequence that caused them to fall has already been unfolding for 18 to 36 months. The real warning signs appear in a specific order, and watching them in that order is what gives you predictive power. The guide frames this as the residential construction cycle, which sits inside what the author calls the Cyclical Economy, the most interest-rate-sensitive part of the system and the first place stress shows up when the Fed tightens.

## What Must Happen, In Order

The sequence begins with the Fed tightening policy. This is the prerequisite. Without a tightening cycle, the chain does not fire. Once tightening is underway, new home sales are the first indicator to respond. Buyers pull back as mortgage rates rise and sales volume drops. This typically happens 18 to 24 months before home prices move. Second, building permits decline. Permits are issued before construction begins, so when both new home sales and permits are falling together for three to six months, the guide says the sequence has officially started. Third, units under construction peak and begin declining as the earlier drop in permits works its way through the pipeline. Fourth, residential construction employment peaks and layoffs begin, because fewer homes being built means fewer workers are needed. This is where the guide says the chain reaction spreads, spending drops, loan defaults rise, and banks tighten credit. Fifth and finally, home prices decline. Sellers resist cutting prices even as every other signal weakens, but once the first four dominoes have fallen, prices eventually follow. The guide gives two specific timing rules. When new home sales and permits have both been declining for three to six months, home prices will usually follow 12 to 24 months later. When construction activity and residential employment are both declining, national home price weakness is typically 12 to 18 months away. The guide also emphasizes that the sequence must unfold in order. If the indicators peak out of sequence, it is noise, not the real cycle.

## How to Build the Visual Indicator in React

Start by pulling the five data series from the FRED API, which is free and returns JSON. You want HSN1F for new home sales, PERMIT for building permits, UNDCONTSA for units under construction, CES2023600001 for residential construction employment, and CSUSHPINSA for Case-Shiller home prices. You also want FEDFUNDS to detect whether the Fed is in a tightening cycle. Fetch each series on mount, cache the response, and refresh monthly since the data only updates once a month. Store each series as an array of date and value pairs in state.

For each of the five housing series, compute four derived values. First, the trailing 24-month peak value and the date it occurred. Second, the current value as a percentage off that peak. Third, the number of months since the peak. Fourth, the three-month and six-month rate of change to confirm direction. For the Fed series, compute whether the current rate is higher than it was 12 months ago, which flags tightening as active.

Define a rule for when a domino has fallen. A signal counts as fallen when it peaked at least three months ago, is currently below that peak by some threshold such as five percent, and has a negative three-month rate of change. Apply this rule to each of the five series in order. Then build a stage score from zero to five. Stage zero is Fed tightening with no dominoes fallen. Stage one is new home sales fallen. Stage two is permits also fallen, which is the guide's first major trigger. Stage three adds units under construction. Stage four adds residential employment, which is the guide's second major trigger. Stage five is home prices declining, meaning the full sequence has completed.

Layer in a sequence integrity check. Compare the peak dates of the fallen dominoes and verify they occurred in the correct order, meaning new home sales peaked before permits, which peaked before units under construction, which peaked before employment, which peaked before home prices. If the peaks are out of order, flag the signal as a false start rather than a valid sequence. Display this as a separate status indicator.

For the visual layout, render the five indicators as a horizontal row of nodes representing the sequence, each node showing its name, whether it has peaked, its percent off peak, and its months since peak. Color each node based on its state, neutral when still expanding, warning when rolling over, and red when fully fallen. Above the row, display the current stage score and the guide's expected timing for home price decline based on which stage has been reached. At stage two, show 12 to 24 months out. At stage four, show 12 to 18 months out. Include the Fed tightening status as a gate indicator above the whole thing, since the sequence is not valid without it. Add the sequence integrity flag nearby so a user can see whether the peaks happened in the correct order. Below the node row, render a small sparkline for each series showing the last 36 months of data with the peak marked, so the user can visually verify the rollover. Update the entire dashboard when new FRED data arrives each month.
